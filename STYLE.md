# Ellingtonia style guide

*Drafted with AI assistance. Inspired by the Chicago Manual of Style and by
user interviews.*

This guide currently covers one topic: the capitalisation of song titles.
Only letter case is covered here. Spelling, punctuation and italics are out
of scope.

The rules are paraphrased from the Chicago Manual of Style, 18th edition
(§§8.159–8.162, 8.164, 11.8, 11.27, 11.30, 13.90 and 13.100), with a few
repository-specific conventions added for things Chicago doesn't cover (such
as catalogue annotations).

## General principles

- Only letter case changes. Spelling, word choice and punctuation in a title
  are kept as they are in the source.
- A title printed in full capitals on a source is set in normal upper and
  lower case, using the rules for its language. Initialisms and acronyms stay
  in capitals.
- Existing capitalisation in the catalogue is not itself a rule. Where a title
  doesn't follow this guide, the guide is what's correct.

## English titles: title case

English titles use title case (also called headline style). Apply these rules:

1. Capitalise the first and last word of a title, and the first and last word
   of any subtitle.
2. Capitalise all other major words: nouns, pronouns, verbs (including forms
   such as "Is" and "Ain't"), adjectives, adverbs, and subordinating
   conjunctions such as *if*, *that*, *when* and *because* (as in *If You
   Can't Hold the Man You Love*).
3. Lowercase the articles *a*, *an* and *the*.
4. Lowercase prepositions of four letters or fewer, such as *in*, *on*, *of*,
   *to*, *for*, *from*, *with*, *till*, *like* and *into*. Capitalise longer
   prepositions, such as *About*, *Above*, *Through*, *Under* and *Without*.
   - Exception: a word that works as an adverb or adjective, rather than a
     preposition, is capitalised, as in *Come Off the Veldt*, *Turn Down* or
     *Look Up*.
5. Lowercase the coordinating conjunctions *and*, *but*, *or*, *nor* and *for*.
6. Lowercase *as* in every use.
7. Lowercase *to* in every use, including *used to* and the infinitive, except
   where it is an adverb (as in *Come To*).
8. Lowercase the parts of a name that would be lowercase in running text, such
   as *da* in *da Vinci* or *van* in *van Gogh*.
9. For hyphenated words, capitalise the first element. Capitalise each later
   element unless it is an article, a short preposition or a coordinating
   conjunction. Prefix compounds such as *Pre-Authorization* follow the same
   rule. Spelled-out numbers capitalise both parts, as in *Ninety-Fifth*.
   Exception: a few titles are hyphenated purely for spelling or pronunciation,
   not as a compound of separate words, and keep their own established
   capitalisation, such as *Chlo-e* (not *Chlo-E*) and *Lonely Co-ed* (not
   *Co-Ed*).
10. A short contraction standing in for a lowercase word, such as *o'* (of),
    *'n'* (and) or *'bout* ('about), is always lowercase, wherever it falls in
    the title: *Lot o' Fingers*, *Riff 'n' Drill*, *Something 'bout Believing*.

Rule 4 uses a four-letter cut-off because Chicago's rule is "fewer than five
letters"; Chicago capitalises prepositions of five letters or more.

Examples:

- Things Ain't What They Used to Be
- Take the "A" Train
- Jam with Sam
- Do Nothin' till You Hear from Me
- I Got It Bad and That Ain't Good
- One More Time for the People
- Diminuendo in Blue
- Come Off the Veldt
- C-Jam Blues

## French, Italian and Spanish titles: sentence case

Titles in French, Italian or Spanish use sentence case, not title case.

1. Capitalise the first word of the title. Capitalise the first word of any
   subtitle that follows a colon.
2. Capitalise anything the language's ordinary prose would capitalise anyway:
   - proper names of people, places, bands, institutions and so on, such as
     *Cherbourg* or *Bulletin de la Société des antiquaires de l'Ouest*
   - nouns of nationality or origin, such as *une Africaine* (a noun: "an
     African woman"). The corresponding adjective stays lowercase, as in
     *africain(e)* describing something else: *La Plus Belle Africaine* is
     *La plus belle africaine*, because there *africaine* describes an
     implied "woman", so it's an adjective, not a noun on its own.
   - Roman numerals, with the ordinal ending kept in lowercase, as in *XXe*
3. Lowercase everything else. That includes the articles and prepositions
   inside a title (*le*, *la*, *les*, *de*, *du*, *des*, *en*, *et*, and
   their Italian/Spanish equivalents), and any *L'* or *Le* that is not the
   first word.
4. Where a title's first word is an elided article (*L'*), the next word is
   lowercase unless rule 2 applies.

Examples from Chicago (11.30):

- L'assommoir
- L'exil et le royaume
- Le père Goriot
- Paris au XXe siècle
- Un cœur simple
- Les Rougon-Macquart (the family name is a proper noun)

And from the catalogue:

- La plus belle africaine
- La dolce vita
- La Virgen de la Macarena

## Mixed and bracketed titles

When a title combines languages, each part follows the rules for its own
language. For example, a French title followed by an English alternative in
square brackets is written as *Le sucrier velour [Do Not Disturb]*. The French
part uses sentence case and the English part uses title case.

This also applies to a foreign phrase sitting inside an otherwise English
title, not just bracketed alternatives: *Jazz à la carte*, *Blues à la Willie
Cook*, *Ultra de luxe*. The foreign words (*à la*, *de*) stay lowercase even
though the rest of the title is in title case; only the title's own first and
last words are capitalised by the English rule.

## Catalogue annotations

Descriptive notes that the catalogue adds, such as *(theme)*, *(take 2)*,
*(master take)*, *(stereo mix)* or a reel/cue reference such as *(reel 3, cue
6)*, are not part of a title. These are lowercase. This is a repository
convention, not a Chicago rule. A trailing code after a hyphen, such as a
missed-vocal marker like *-TT,TW* or a take code like *-tkDE*, is left exactly
as in the source rather than cased at all.

Words that are part of the title as issued or registered, such as the
bracketed *[Harlem Twist]*, follow the rules above.

A catalogue identifier such as *New DESOR* is not touched by these rules at
all; it's fixed reference text, not a title. (Inconsistent abbreviations of
it, such as *NDESOR*, are a data consistency issue, not a capitalisation one.)
